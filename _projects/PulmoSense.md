Heart failure affects an extensive 6.7 million Americans and sends roughly 1 million to the hospital each year, witb 1 in 4 readmitted within a month. Despite the fact that pulmonary congestion builds two to four days, patients can't feel it, and today's in-home monitoring is merely reactive. Preventative measures are expnesive and only possible in the hospital. 

I led a team to build the full, market-ready business case for PulmoSense, a wearable vest that uses a FLEX-licensed technology to watch lung fluid continuously from home and stream readings straight to medical software accessible by your doctor, enabling an estimated 35% earlier detection of a failure event at 64-89% less cost than existing options.

## Approach

Over the course of the semester, through extensive interviews, meetings, and customer discovery, we developed a vast array of resources of documentation and planning for the business. These documents were reviewed and verified by our professor for accuracy, viability, and presentation. 

## Result

1) 50+ stakeholder interviews across cardiologists, hospital value analysis committees, nurses, payers, and patients, used to map a four-role buying committee and reposition the value proposition around continuous, objective lung-fluid data
2) One-pager investor brief synthesizing the full opportunity, technology, and go-to-market into a single deliverable ready for Pre-Seed conversations
3) IP and defensibility strategy anchored on the exclusive FLEX patent license (WO 2023/076593 A1) for RF lung-fluid sensing, plus proprietary signal-processing and calibration methods
4) Market sizing with defended TAM ($2.0B), SAM ($850M), and SOM ($33.4M by Year 5 across 240 hospitals)
5) Business Model Canvas iterated across five check-ins, with five key partners and multiple priced revenue streams 
6) Unit economics and financial model yielding a 4.9x LTV:CAC ratio; hospital-level economics of ~$108K revenue against ~$99.5K CAC per hospital; cash-flow positive Year 2, cumulative profitability Year 4
7) Three-channel go-to-market strategy (KOL seeding → physician champions → VAC procurement) with a tiered $400K–$800K annual channel budget
8) Prototype scope at $306K with itemized BOM tied to a Month-6 build target and Month-9 FDA Pre-Submission
9) Regulatory and capital plan: $900K Pre-Seed (Launch NY, NIH SBIR Phase I, AHA, Southern Tier REDC) → $1.5–3M Seed → $4–8M Series A, mapped to IDE clinical study (Months 6–21) and 510(k) clearance with first paying hospital by Month 30
10) Final investor-style presentation awarded perfect final score by a panel of investors
11) Successfully hand-picked and managed a team of extremely diverse engineering and business backgrounds, and managed coordination, running meetings, leading presentations, and managing conflict.

## What I would change

In the future, I would ensure that communication channels for the team are optimized with expectations on frequency of messages and updates. I would also bring in advisors earlier into the process and estabilish a tighter connection with them.
